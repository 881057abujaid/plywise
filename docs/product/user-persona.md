## 1. Persona Overview

### The Self-Directed Improving Player

The primary PlyWise user is a chess player who understands the basic rules of chess and can independently play complete games but struggles to convert repeated gameplay into consistent improvement.

The player is motivated to become better.

They play games, recognize that they make mistakes, and may occasionally use engine analysis or post-game review tools.

However, they often struggle to understand the deeper reason behind their decisions and recurring errors.

The primary user does not necessarily lack access to chess information.

The user lacks continuous, contextual guidance that connects their own decisions to meaningful learning.

## 3. Current Behavior

The player primarily improves through repeated gameplay.

A common learning cycle is:

1. Play a chess game.
2. Win or lose.
3. Open post-game analysis.
4. Review move classifications.
5. Inspect one or more blunders or mistakes.
6. Look at the engine's suggested move.
7. Understand some of the analysis.
8. Start another game.

The player may recognize individual bad moves.

However, the player often fails to connect those moves into a recurring behavioral pattern.

For example, the player may repeatedly:

- ignore opponent threats,
- attack before completing development,
- expose the king,
- move the queen unnecessarily,
- overlook tactical captures,
- lose material through poor board awareness,
- or make impulsive moves in unfamiliar positions.

The player experiences these as separate mistakes across separate games.

A mentor may recognize them as a pattern.

---

## 4. Core Problem

The primary user frequently receives technically correct chess information without enough personalized learning context.

An engine may communicate:

> The move was a blunder.

The player still wants to understand:

> What did I fail to notice?

> Why do I keep making this kind of mistake?

> What should I check before making a similar move?

> Was my idea wrong, or was my execution wrong?

> How can I recognize this situation next time?

The player's problem is therefore not limited to move accuracy.

The deeper problem is the gap between **engine evaluation** and **decision understanding**.

---

## 5. Goals

The primary user wants to:

- make fewer recurring mistakes,
- understand important mistakes in simple chess language,
- recognize tactical and positional warning signals,
- improve decision-making during games,
- understand personal playing tendencies,
- learn from actual games rather than only abstract theory,
- practice important chess concepts interactively,
- and become less dependent on external move suggestions over time.

The player's long-term goal is not to memorize every engine line.

The player wants to think more effectively without assistance.

---

## 6. Motivations

### 6.1 Visible Improvement

The player wants evidence that repeated play and learning are producing improvement.

Improvement may be reflected through better decisions, fewer recurring mistakes, stronger pattern recognition, or changes in playing performance.

### 6.2 Understanding

The player values explanations that make a position or mistake understandable.

A clear explanation can be more useful to this player than a long engine variation.

### 6.3 Practical Learning

The player prefers learning that connects directly to chess decisions.

Interactive positions, guided lessons, and feedback based on attempted moves are more relevant than passive information alone.

### 6.4 Independence

The player ultimately wants to make stronger decisions independently.

The user may appreciate coaching, but does not want to remain permanently dependent on being told the best move.

### 6.5 Personal Relevance

The player is more motivated when feedback reflects mistakes and tendencies visible in their own games.

Generic chess advice may be useful.

Advice connected to the player's actual behavior is more meaningful.

---

## 7. Frustrations

The primary user may experience the following frustrations:

- engine evaluations feel numerical rather than instructional,
- suggested moves are difficult to understand,
- long engine lines create information overload,
- post-game analysis happens after the original thought process has faded,
- the same type of mistake appears repeatedly,
- generic lessons do not always feel connected to actual weaknesses,
- chess improvement feels unstructured,
- and professional coaching may not be continuously accessible.

The player may know that they are playing poorly without knowing what to systematically improve.

---

## 8. Learning Characteristics

The primary PlyWise user learns best when:

- feedback is connected to a recent decision,
- explanations focus on the important chess idea,
- the amount of information is controlled,
- the player is allowed to attempt a move before receiving guidance,
- recurring patterns are explicitly identified,
- improvement advice is practical,
- and the system encourages the player to think rather than immediately revealing answers.

The player does not require every move to be explained.

Excessive intervention may reduce focus and create dependence.

Selective coaching is therefore important to the persona.

---

## 9. Relationship with Chess Engines

The player may already know that chess engines are stronger than human players.

The user does not need PlyWise to prove engine strength.

The player needs help interpreting engine-backed evidence.

The engine may identify:

- evaluation changes,
- tactical opportunities,
- stronger moves,
- and technical inaccuracies.

PlyWise should use this information to support mentoring.

The primary persona should not be expected to independently interpret raw engine output, centipawn changes, deep principal variations, or complex technical analysis.

These details may exist internally without defining the primary user experience.

---

## 10. Relationship with the PlyWise Coach

The PlyWise Coach should feel like an attentive chess mentor.

The coach observes the player's decisions and intervenes selectively.

The coach should:

- establish a mentoring presence,
- identify meaningful learning moments,
- explain relevant mistakes,
- recognize positive decisions where useful,
- connect recurring behaviors across games,
- and provide practical improvement guidance.

The coach should not behave like:

- a general-purpose chatbot,
- a constant move announcer,
- an engine terminal,
- an exaggerated motivational assistant,
- or an infallible psychological profiler.

The coach should distinguish between observed evidence and uncertain interpretation.

---

## 11. Lesson Mode Needs

The primary persona benefits from guided Lesson Mode because recognizing a weakness is not always enough to correct it.

For example, identifying repeated king-safety mistakes does not automatically teach the player how to evaluate king safety.

Lesson Mode should allow the player to interact with focused chess situations.

The player should be able to:

- understand the lesson objective,
- inspect a relevant chess position,
- make a decision,
- receive feedback on the attempted move,
- understand the key concept,
- and progress through the lesson.

Lessons should prioritize active decision-making where appropriate.

The primary persona is more likely to retain a concept after attempting to apply it than after only reading about it.

## 12. Typical User Scenario

A player starts a game against the PlyWise computer opponent.

The Coach establishes its presence at the beginning of the game.

The player begins normally.

During the game, the player makes an aggressive queen move.

The move is not immediately catastrophic, but it contributes to a recurring pattern previously observed in the player's games.

The Coach Engine determines that the decision represents a meaningful learning opportunity.

The coach provides a short contextual message explaining that the player is committing the queen early while development remains incomplete.

Later, the player makes a tactical mistake and loses material.

The coach identifies the immediate issue and provides guidance relevant to the missed board signal.

The game continues.

At the end of the game, PlyWise does not simply report two mistakes and one blunder.

The coach explains that the player's aggressive intentions created active possibilities, but repeated early queen movement and missed opponent threats weakened the position.

The post-game guidance identifies a practical improvement focus.

The player may later use Lesson Mode to practice a related concept through guided positions.

Across future games, PlyWise observes whether the same behavior continues.

## 13. Primary Jobs to Be Done

When I make a meaningful chess mistake,

> I want to understand what I failed to notice,

so that I can recognize a similar situation in the future.

When I repeatedly make similar mistakes,

> I want those mistakes to be connected into a pattern,

so that I know what part of my chess thinking needs improvement.

When I am learning a chess concept,

> I want to make decisions in relevant positions,

so that I can practice applying the idea rather than only reading about it.

When I finish a game,

> I want feedback based on how I actually played,

so that I leave with a practical improvement focus.

---

## 14. Persona Success Outcome

PlyWise successfully serves the primary persona when the player begins to independently recognize chess signals that previously required external explanation.

The desired progression is:

**Unaware → Explained → Practiced → Recognized → Independently Applied**

The strongest evidence of product value is not that the player asks the Coach more questions.

It is that the player gradually needs fewer explanations for previously recurring mistakes.

---

## 15. Persona Boundary

The primary MVP persona is not:

- a complete chess beginner who does not understand legal piece movement,
- a titled or elite player requiring deep professional preparation,
- a player primarily seeking competitive tournament infrastructure,
- a user seeking social chess networking,
- or a user primarily interested in raw engine experimentation.

These users may have valid chess needs.

They do not define the primary PlyWise MVP experience.
